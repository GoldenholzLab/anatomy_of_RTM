Findings, ranked by scientific importance

R1 | Moderate | Plausible scientific interpretation risk, supported by confirmed simulator configuration

Methods 2.1, P027, describes CHOCOLATES as a simulator of "heterogeneous seizure rates, clustering, cycles and the association between frequency and variability." Limitations, P122, says daily sampling cannot address within-day cycles. The actual configuration is much more restricted than that qualification makes apparent. In the local simulator, simple_CHOCOLATES calls simulator_base with sampRATE=1. make_cycles excludes the 12-hour and 24-hour components; the weekly component has amplitude zero; the 4-to-45-day component has selection probability zero. The only default nonzero cycle retained is a 90-to-450-day component, selected with probability 0.011649. Thus 98.8351% of generated histories have no explicit nonzero cyclic modulation under these defaults. This statement concerns the generator's cycle waveform, not an absence of random temporal fluctuation or clustering.

Code evidence is [realSim_turbo.py:25](/Users/dgoldenh/Documents/GitHub/CHOCOLATE/realSim_turbo.py:25), [realSim_turbo.py:295](/Users/dgoldenh/Documents/GitHub/CHOCOLATE/realSim_turbo.py:295), and [realSim_turbo.py:311](/Users/dgoldenh/Documents/GitHub/CHOCOLATE/realSim_turbo.py:311). The relevant ranges are 295-308 and 311-344. The manuscript calls this source through [rtm123.py:163](/Users/dgoldenh/Documents/GitHub/anatomy_of_RTM/rtm_anatomy/rtm123.py:163). A new sample of 1,024 retained patient seeds regenerated exactly; only 10 had nonzero cycle waveforms. See document_and_strata_results.json, cycles.

Why it matters: temporal dependence influences rolling eligibility and subsequent change. Readers should not assume that this experiment retained the published model's full range of seizure cycles. The original CHOCOLATES paper reports a configurable model and validation of particular simulation settings, which does not itself validate every later configuration. [Original CHOCOLATES study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36401798/).

Minimal corrective action: add the actual daily simulator settings to the supplementary methods and explicitly state the absence of weekly and 4-to-45-day modulation under these defaults. If the authors intend to claim robustness to realistic multidien cycling, a separate sensitivity analysis is needed. It need not replace the current saved cohort or results. The present narrower descriptive conclusion can remain if its scope is clear.

Verification performed: source inspection, exact probability derivation from the defaults, and 1,024 seed-level regeneration checks with returned cycle arrays. Not established: how the headline RTM and Type 3 percentages would change under other cycle distributions, or whether the daily configuration has separate empirical validation. I do not treat the current configuration as proof of incorrect arithmetic or invalidation of the operational definitions.

Mathematical and methodological checks

The operational RTM rule is internally consistent. Let B be the mean monthly count in the two baseline months, T the mean monthly count in the three test months, and R the relevant finite-history reference. The rule is B > R and B - R > |T - R|. An algebraically equivalent rule is T < B and B + T > 2R. I used the latter form with integer totals as an independent full-array check. Every selected person's measured and true classifications agreed. Exact ties fail. This matches the operational equation in the cited prior RTM paper. [Goldenholz et al., 2023, definition of RTM](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10592227/).

Here R for true counts is the condition-specific true total divided by 36. The measured reference is sensitivity times R. Monthly observed counts are independently thinned true counts plus Poisson alarms, then the expected monthly alarm count is subtracted and the result floored at zero. Weekly correction retains 30/7 rather than rounding to 4. The daily interval rule separately subtracts rounded expected daily alarms. Code tracing at rtm123.py lines 662-705 and independent raw-history replay agree with Methods P032. The first selected baseline start ranges from 0 to 31 inclusive. Each has two baseline months and three subsequent test months. Eligibility requires mean at least 4 or 8, each baseline month at least 3, and no run of more than 25 zero days within the 60-day baseline. I checked accepted starts, rejected earlier starts, late eligible windows, ineligible histories, and fixed zero baselines in the new stratified replay.

The four paired classes partition each selected population. No second true-count eligibility restriction is applied. All primary selected baselines are positive. Perfect detection gives identical true and measured classifications and zero Type 3, including in the Type 1 and Type 2 conditions. Measurement can remove a true RTM classification as well as add one, and the manuscript describes that correctly.

Type 1 and Type 2 are condition contrasts, not individual membership classes parallel to the Type 3 disagreement category. Neither the 58.3% transient-condition RTM nor the 57.9% threshold-condition RTM estimates an isolated causal contribution of its named mechanism. The manuscript now makes this distinction clearly. The Type 1 replacement resamples more than the mean, and the original daily series resumes after the replacement. Figure 1 is an illustration of upward temporary worsening, not a plot of the pooled bidirectional replacement experiment; its caption identifies the examples as illustrations. I found no mathematical inconsistency in its rasterized rule or Type 3 example.

The confidence intervals are marginal Monte Carlo intervals across independent synthetic patients. Reusing a cohort across conditions does not invalidate these marginal intervals. A paired contrast interval would require additional covariance-aware calculations, but the paper does not claim such inferential contrasts. There are no hypothesis tests needing multiplicity correction. The million-person sample produces high conditional numerical precision, not evidence that the model is clinically calibrated.

The detector model separately varies sensitivity and false alarms. It does not estimate the interaction of imperfect sensitivity with nonzero false alarms, time-varying performance, false-alarm clustering, or informative missingness. This is a restriction of the stated one-input-at-a-time design, not an implementation mismatch.

Limitations already adequately disclosed

The following should remain visible, but I do not count them as newly discovered errors requiring correction: a finite internal reference that includes the trial window; monthly flooring that raises expected corrected counts relative to the sensitivity-scaled reference; the unusual rounded daily correction; imperfect empirical diary calibration treated as biological truth; non-data-derived transient replacements; detector performance constant across people and time; post hoc paired analysis; lack of treatment effects, randomized contrasts, power calculations, or causal decomposition; and limited transportability to other disorders and trial designs.

As a bounded sensitivity diagnostic, I recomputed the classifiers from saved sufficient statistics after omitting the five trial months from the reference, keeping enrollment and all observed counts unchanged. Perfect-detector RTM changed from 53.78895% to 53.40072%; FAR 1/day Type 3 changed from 41.56828% to 41.40306%. This check does not negate reference dependence and does not establish robustness to an independent external diary or an expectation-matched measurement reference. It supports the narrower statement that this particular internal-reference modification does not explain away the large reported measurement-only category. Full diagnostic results are in independent_audit_results.json.

The handling of placebo/context claims is also appropriately qualified. The manuscript does not infer that psychological effects are absent in real epilepsy trials. Its asthma example separates objective from subjective outcomes and explicitly says the untreated comparison did not isolate RTM. The primary study supports that distinction. [Wechsler et al., original asthma experiment](https://dash.harvard.edu/entities/publication/73120379-2e3d-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b). The cited wearable simulation supports evaluating devices within an intended use case and using expected-alarm corrections; it does not validate this paper's specific rolling selection, daily correction, or paired Type 3 percentages. [Goldenholz et al., wearable simulation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11018505/).
