Critical review resolution
November 13, 2026

Opus 5.5, Astra, Sol 6.1 and Fable 5.2 completed their requested reviews. All four found that the reported primary numbers agree with the saved results. Their independent replays also agreed with the saved classifications. Each ran the same 23 existing unit tests successfully; repeated use of that test suite is shared evidence, not four independent validations of the scientific model.

My assessment is that the primary calculations can stand, but the manuscript needs material reporting changes before submission. The operational definitions are coherent under the stated model. Their numerical prevalences depend on enrollment, screening, correction and reference choices. They do not identify additive causal mechanisms, clinical detector effects, or trial efficiency.

The original manuscript, supplement, existing analysis code and saved primary outputs remain unchanged. The revised review copies are separate files in consolidated_draft, with filenames ending in _critical_review.docx. Both were visually checked and saved in Microsoft Word. The decision ledger is resolution_ledger.json. It maps every numbered finding from all four reviews to the decisions below.

1. Detector-selected enrollment at high false-alarm rates

Accepted as a material interpretation issue. Applying the original entry rule to true counts in the same detector-selected window gives the following full-cohort results at one false alarm/day. Of 985,321 detector-selected participants, 391,290 meet the true-count rule. Type 3 occurs in 61,303 of those participants, or 15.7%, and in 348,278 of the 594,031 who fail it. The latter group supplies 85.0% of the 409,581 primary Type 3 cases.

The 41.6% primary prevalence is correct for the detector-selected population. Substituting 15.7% would change the population and answer a different question. The revised abstract specifies the detector-selected denominator and reports the 85.0% composition. Results and Table S7 give the split and its denominators. These data describe composition; they do not prove that a particular fraction of Type 3 is caused by false enrollment.

2. Flooring and the measured reference

Accepted as a material sensitivity, rather than a demonstrated programming error. The primary reference follows the Methods. It is not the conditional expected value of corrected measured counts after flooring at zero.

I calculated that alternative reference over the complete retained cohort, holding enrollment, windows, measured counts and true RTM labels fixed. At one false alarm/day, measured RTM changes from 69.9% to 63.6%, and Type 3 changes from 41.6% to 37.3%. The alternative reference is 1.09 events/month higher on average among selected participants. Table S8 reports all three false-alarm settings. The revised Results identifies this dependence. The alternative changes the estimand and does not replace the primary result or quantify model uncertainty.

3. Classification can change in either direction

Accepted. The revised abstract pairs the sensitivity-70% Type 3 prevalence of 7.7% with 4.8% true-only RTM. Table S3 now reports true-only prevalence and the net measured-minus-true difference for fixed enrollment. In that fixed population at 70% sensitivity, measured-only and true-only prevalences are 5.4% and 9.1%, giving a net reduction of 3.7 percentage points. A measured-only prevalence is not the net amount of RTM added by a detector.

4. First and later eligible screens

Accepted. Of the 649,098 perfect-detector enrollees, 330,389 qualify at the first screen and have 38.3% RTM and 14.6% median percentage reduction. The remaining 318,709 qualify later and have 69.8% RTM and 40.0% median reduction. Results and Tables S9 and S10 now show the screening split. The limitations describe up to 32 monthly screens and distinguish this design from a single prospective baseline.

The split helps explain why related designs yield different numbers. It does not isolate a causal effect of rescreening, since the groups differ in who qualifies and when. Medians cannot be decomposed by averaging subgroup medians.

5. Random-window comparator

Accepted as descriptive context. I drew one start month uniformly from 0 to 31 for each perfect-detector enrollee, using seed 20261113 and no window-level eligibility check. Operational RTM was 25.7%. Median reduction was 0.0% among 641,362 positive baselines; 7,736 zero baselines were excluded from that median only. Table S11 gives the comparison.

I do not accept the stronger interpretation that subtracting this rate from 53.8% estimates a causal contribution of selection, or that the original 53.8% is an arithmetic overstatement. Membership is still selected, and random windows need not meet the trial rule. The revised manuscript makes this distinction explicit.

6. Transient changes, cycles and uncertainty reporting

Accepted. Table S4 contains every overlap and direction stratum, including subgroup sizes and the never-eligible group. The text distinguishes the bidirectional experiment from the upward-change illustration. The sampled rate-ratio distribution has 5th and 95th percentiles 0.07 and 14.41. In the baseline-only overlap group, 77.8% never qualify without the transient. These are model-defined selected strata, not estimates of clinical transient worsening.

The supplementary methods describe the daily cycle configuration. Weekly amplitude and 4-to-45-day selection probability are zero; the retained 90-to-450-day component has probability 0.011649. Most histories therefore lack an explicit cycle waveform, although stochastic variability and clustering remain. Robustness to other cycle distributions has not been established.

Tables S5 and S6 report the computed primary and fixed-enrollment median intervals. A note explains tied, discrete percentage-change values and repeated or collapsed median intervals. I retained the existing median estimator. Changing it to an interpolated statistic solely to avoid ties is unnecessary. These intervals describe Monte Carlo precision under the model, not clinical or model uncertainty.

7. Method details and presentation

Accepted. The draft now states that sensitivity acts through independent binomial thinning and that detected counts are not divided by sensitivity before applying entry thresholds. The daily rounded alarm subtraction is zero at one alarm/month and one alarm/week. Window starts are months 0 through 31. The 25-day limit follows the Krauss trial, whereas Chung used 21 days; this analysis uses 30-day months instead of four-week segments.

Supplement condition names now match Table 1. The undefined mSF abbreviation was removed. Captions explain the Figure 1 type labels, its full-sensitivity reference illustration, and Figure 2 condition-family colors. Notes explain independent rounding and the discrete medians. The two distinct quantities rounding to 41.6% are retained with their denominators; numerical coincidence is not an error. A live citation to the existing reference 1 was added to the Introduction sentence about prior seizure-trial simulations.

8. Related work

Accepted as an important omission. Primary records verify three relevant works, including a published software re-review that reversed the earlier efficiency conclusion. It supports the manuscript's caution that reducing apparent RTM need not improve simulated trial efficiency. The two medRxiv records are preprints, and the earlier anatomy preprint is a prior version of this manuscript rather than an independent study.

The author approved importing both verified preprints into My Library, anatomy_of_RTM. Both imports were verified in that collection. The published paper was already in the local Zotero library. All three related works now have live citations in the review copy, and Zotero refreshed them and the 21-entry bibliography through Microsoft Word.

9. Reproducibility and author details

Accepted as a release requirement. The exact current external simulator source is preserved locally with hashes and an implementation snapshot. The primary analysis source hash agrees with its run manifest. Successful sampled regenerations show current compatibility with retained histories. They do not prove the identity of the historical generator or recover its original environment. The historical fingerprint remains unknown.

The local snapshot is not a public immutable archive, and it does not contain the full retained cohort. Public reproduction still requires an obtainable version of the exact simulator, its license, revised analysis, retained inputs or a documented regeneration route, outputs and numerical environment. No commit, push, public data deposit or publication was performed. The availability text continues to say public release is pending.
